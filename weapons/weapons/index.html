Drop Command Weapons & Units: Strengths, Weaknesses, Best Use

Drop Command's combined-arms combat only works when each unit type does the job it was built for. This guide breaks down all five unit families — Mechs, Tanks, VTOLs, Infantry and Combat Armor — with the same template: strengths, weaknesses, and the situation each one is best used in. Sources checked October 18, 2026 against the current build.

Mechs — your line breakers

Strengths: heaviest armor on the field, survives focus fire that deletes anything smaller, and anchors a push.
Weaknesses: slow to reposition and expensive to replace — losing one mid-campaign hurts your economy, not just the battle.
Best use: lead the assault on fortified positions while cheaper units screen for anti-armor threats. Do not send a Mech in alone; combined arms exists to protect it.

Tanks — the flexible backbone

Strengths: the best cost-to-durability ratio, fast enough to respond across a front, and effective against both structures and medium targets.
Weaknesses: vulnerable to dedicated anti-armor and to being flanked in tight terrain.
Best use: the default answer for most fights — form the main line, exploit breaches, and let Mechs tank the heaviest fire. When in doubt, bring more tanks.

VTOLs — raiders and eyes

Strengths: ignore terrain entirely, strike rear-line assets (artillery, supply, undefended extractors) and scout ahead of the main force.
Weaknesses: thin armor — any dedicated anti-air turns them into credits confetti.
Best use: harassment and intelligence. Send VTOLs where the enemy is weakest, not where the fight is thickest; retreat them the moment anti-air appears.

Infantry — cheap, everywhere, underestimated

Strengths: lowest cost, garrisons captures territory, and holds ground that armor would waste on.
Weaknesses: dies in droves to any area effect; useless in open-field charges against armor.
Best use: territory control and garrison duty behind the line. Infantry wins the map while tanks win the battle — campaigns are scored on the map.

Combat Armor — the specialist

Strengths: bridges the gap between infantry flexibility and mech durability; strong in close engagements where tanks struggle to bring guns to bear.
Weaknesses: jack-of-all-trades cost profile — pricier than infantry, less punch than a Mech.
Best use: urban and rough-terrain fights, and as a flexible reserve that can reinforce either flank.

Combining them: the battle line that works

The core combined-arms pattern: Mechs absorb and break, Tanks deal and take the mid-weight damage, VTOLs cut the rear, Infantry takes and holds the ground behind them, and Combat Armor plugs whichever gap the enemy attacks. If your composition is one unit family, you do not have an army — you have a vulnerability list.
